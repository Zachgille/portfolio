Bracket optimizer

A Python and DuckDB pipeline for March Madness pools. It looks for the bracket most likely to win your pool, which usually isn’t the most likely bracket.

Stack
Python, DuckDB, pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn
Scale
50,000 simulated tournaments per run by default
Source
github.com/Zachgille/march-madness-optimizer

The question

Pick the likelier team in every game and you get the bracket with the most expected points. But a pool doesn’t score you against reality alone. It ranks you against everyone else, and plenty of them made the same picks. If your champion wins and a third of the pool picked it too, you’re one of many contenders for first. A less likely champion that few others picked can be worth more.

The real evaluator works with whole brackets rather than one pick, but the effect is the same one.

The pipeline

  1. Reconcile teamsEvery data source names teams differently, so a crosswalk maps names and provider IDs to one ID before anything else runs.
  2. Build matchup features18 pregame features per game: seeds, adjusted offense and defense, net rating, tempo, strength of schedule, recent form, neutral-court rating and the four factors.
  3. Train a logistic modelFit on 4,502 historical games and tested on 668 held out. Its probabilities can then be blended with betting-market odds in log-odds space, weighted 70% market and 30% model by default.
  4. Simulate the tournament50,000 runs by default, giving every team’s odds of reaching each round.
  5. Score candidate bracketsEach candidate plays against a simulated pool. Opponents are drawn mostly from public pick rates (75% public, 25% true odds, plus noise) in four styles: public, balanced, chalky and contrarian. The output compares expected points, chance of finishing first, expected payout and how much each bracket overlaps the field.

Your pool, your settings

Pool size and payouts are environment variables, so the same pipeline can answer for a ten-person office pool or a two-hundred-person one. Round scoring defaults to 10, 20, 40, 80, 160 and 320 points.
