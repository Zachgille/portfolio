March Madness
Bracket Optimizer

An end-to-end modeling pipeline that carries uncertainty from team data through tournament simulations to pool-specific bracket decisions.

DATA TO DECISION

A prediction is
one stage of the system.

The pipeline makes each transformation explicit: reconcile identities, generate features, train a model, simulate tournaments, then evaluate bracket strategy. Select a stage to explore.

01 / Prepare

Start with consistent team identities.

The pipeline ingests NCAA and tournament data from multiple sources and reconciles team names and aliases before generating matchup features. Reproducible stages connect ingestion to modeling and evaluation.

02 / Model

Estimate matchups from basketball data.

The active logistic model uses 18 matchup features, including seeds, adjusted offense and defense, net rating, tempo, strength of schedule, recent form, neutral-court rating, and four-factor statistics.

03 / Simulate

Propagate uncertainty through the bracket.

Monte Carlo runs turn matchup probabilities into round-reach, Final Four, and championship estimates. The recorded pipeline produced 4,556 current matchup predictions and ran 50,000 tournament simulations.

04 / Optimize

Choose for the scoring environment.

Candidate brackets are evaluated by expected points, simulated first-place probability, expected payout, and public-pick duplication. The objective depends on the pool, not only on the most likely winner of each game.

THE PROBLEM

A likely winner is only
part of a good bracket.

Tournament predictions answer which team is more likely to win a matchup. Bracket selection adds a different question: how does a complete set of picks perform under the pool’s scoring, payout, and competing selections?

I built a reproducible Python and DuckDB pipeline to connect those questions. After reconciling team identities, the system generates matchup features, trains a logistic model, produces probabilities, and evaluates candidate brackets against simulated tournaments.

EVALUATION OBJECTIVES

Four views of a candidate bracket

Expected points
Average scoring performance over simulated outcomes.
First-place probability
How often the bracket leads the modeled pool.
Expected payout
Performance under the pool’s reward structure.
Public-pick duplication
How much the bracket overlaps with modeled public selections.

ENGINEERING TAKEAWAY

Connect the model
to the actual decision.

The system’s useful output is a way to compare candidate strategies under uncertainty. Feature engineering and holdout evaluation matter, but so do consistent identities, reproducible data stages, and a decision objective that reflects the pool.

Scope of the results

Metrics come from recorded project results and my résumé. No comparison to a baseline model, realized pool winnings, or tournament prediction accuracy is claimed. Simulation estimates depend on the model and pool assumptions. Source code is not published on this site.
